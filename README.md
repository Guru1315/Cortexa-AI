## The brief

Cortexa is a fictional AI company that uses existing CCTV to keep crowds safe at railway stations, stadiums and festivals, and that also builds classroom tools. Its audience is station managers, event organisers and school heads, not engineers. The job of these carousels is to **turn "computer vision" into something you understand in five swipes**: see, count, predict, act.

Every carousel follows the same shape: **a hook on the cover, one idea per slide, real data shown visually, and a clear call to action on the last slide.**

> *"We count people. Not faces."*

## Brand identity

<p align="center"><img src="brand/logo.svg" alt="Cortexa AI logo" width="460"></p>

**The mark** is a hexagon holding a small neural network, with the central node lit in signal lime: a camera's view of a crowd, reduced to connected points. The lowercase wordmark ends in a lime full stop that marks the moment the AI *knows* ([logo on photo](brand/logo-on-photo.webp)).

### Type

- **[Unbounded](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Unbounded)** (700): headlines. Wide, confident and technical without being cold.
- **[Space Grotesk](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Space+Grotesk)**: body copy and UI cards.
- **[JetBrains Mono](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/JetBrains+Mono)**: step labels (`01 — SEE`), slide counters and detection tags, the "machine's voice".

### The rules the carousels follow

1. **Cover = hook.** One question or promise, with the key word in lime.
2. **One step per slide**, numbered in mono (`01 — SEE`, `02 — COUNT`, `03 — PREDICT`).
3. **Show the data, don't describe it.** Every claim gets a chart, a card or a detection box over a real photo.
4. **Lime means "the AI saw this".** It's used only for detections, alerts and the single word that matters.
5. **Last slide = one action**, as a lime pill button.
